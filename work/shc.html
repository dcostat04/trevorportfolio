Case study 02 · SHC Growth Partners

How many deals can one advisor run?

I spent a summer on the analyst side of deals and kept running into the same ceiling: the work that grows with every mandate was done by hand. So I designed a system to lift it.

Role
Summer Intern, Investment Banking
Timeline
April to June 2026
What I made
One working tool, one system design
Status
Design pitched to the firm; not deployed

The day job

Most of my summer was analyst work on live deals and pitches.

I built and validated DCF and financial models, with comparable companies and precedent transactions, ran scenario and sensitivity analysis on them, and prepared pitch decks. I managed virtual data rooms and coordinated stakeholders, and supported cross-border M&A and equity and debt fundraising through market research and buyer identification.

That work taught me where an advisory firm's time actually goes. Valuation is skilled work and should stay with people. Matching each mandate to the right investors, and writing to each of them individually, grows with every new deal, and it was all done by hand.

What I builtA tool that worked

Bulk investor emailing through Zoho Mail

A Chrome extension for bulk investor emailing through Zoho Mail. Each email is personalised to its recipient, with duplicate checks and pacing built in. It is in production use at the firm, and it was the first thing that made me ask the bigger question.

What I designedThe bigger question

If outreach could be automated, how much of the rest of a mandate could be?

On my own time I designed a system with three parts. Each one takes a repeated judgement that an advisor makes by hand and turns it into something the system handles, so the advisor's time goes to the decisions that need a person.

Why start with compliance

In a regulated business, compliance checks are what the rest of the process waits on. Encoding the routine ones as rules means a deal only reaches a person when something is genuinely unusual, the same idea as Order Desk asking about "Belgian" instead of guessing.

Outcome

I presented the design to my manager before the internship ended, and it was well received by the firm.

To be precise: this was pitched, not deployed. "50 deals instead of 5" is the capacity the system was designed for, not a result I measured.

Looking back

Finance taught me how deals are valued. Building for a deal team taught me something more useful for product work: the expensive part of a professional's day is rarely the hard part. It's the repeated part.
