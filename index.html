Icestasy · Jun 2026 to now · Product Management Intern

Replacing a WhatsApp-run business without asking customers to change

Icestasy sells ice cream to cafés, hotels, retailers and distributors across six regions. When I joined, almost every order arrived as a WhatsApp message. A salesperson retyped each one, the kitchen learned what to make when someone told them, and stock planning was largely estimation.

The pitch

The obvious answer was a customer ordering app. I argued against it. Our customers were comfortable on WhatsApp, and asking them to switch would have cost us orders. I proposed to the owner that we keep WhatsApp and rebuild everything that happens after the message arrives.

What I built

Order Desk. A salesperson pastes the message and an AI parser turns it into a structured order, with the right products, region-based pricing for six regions and a GST invoice.

Production and inventory. Orders feed the kitchen and factory directly. Each week the system looks back over six weeks of orders, works out what each flavour and ingredient needs, and flags anything with less than about two and a half weeks of cover.

HRMS. Attendance, leave and approvals, now live for 18 employees, with the rest of a 40+ team being onboarded.

A decision I'd defend

When a message says "Belgian", it could mean Belgian Speculoos or Belgian Chocolate. Order Desk never guesses. It shows the salesperson both and lets them choose. And if the AI service goes down, a rule-based fallback keeps orders moving. A slow order is an inconvenience; a wrong delivery costs a customer.

Result

70–100 orders a day now go through Order Desk. Entering one takes under two minutes instead of 10–15. Manual processing time across the business is down 40%, and stock-outs and wastage have fallen.

What I'd change

I would agree on success metrics before launch. I measured order time carefully, but tracked the drop in stock-outs only informally, which makes the strongest part of the story the hardest to prove.

I led it end to end on my own: requirements, process design, build, testing, rollout and training, using AI-assisted engineering to move faster than a small company normally could.

SHC Growth Partners · Apr to Jun 2026 · Summer Intern, Investment Banking

Asking how many deals one advisor could run

I spent the summer on the analyst side of deals: DCF and comparable valuations for live mandates, virtual data rooms, and buyer research for cross-border M&A. The work that didn't scale was matching each mandate to the right investors and writing every outreach email by hand.

What I built

A small Chrome tool that sends individually personalised investor emails through Zoho Mail, with duplicate checks and pacing so nothing reads like a mass mailing.

What I designed

On my own time, a larger system: a rules engine that encodes compliance checks so routine decisions are automatic, an algorithm that matches investors to deals, and a seven-stage pipeline to track every mandate. It was designed so one advisor could run 50 deals instead of 5.

Outcome

I presented it to my manager before the internship ended, and it was well received by the firm's leadership.

Barclays, Pune · Jul 2023 to Sep 2025 · Graduate Analyst, then Software Engineer (BA4)

Two years where a missed alert meant an outage

I worked in storage infrastructure, where problems surface either early or expensively. The team relied on manual checks and on alerts so noisy that people had learned to ignore them.

What I did

Built alerting for storage arrays, starting with the three or four that mattered most so the team would trust it, then added observability with automated anomaly detection. I automated more than 60% of remediation workflows and the production storage data handling that used to take 30 hours of manual work every week.

Result

Incident response time fell 48% and downtime 30%. Data accuracy improved 38%. I was promoted to BA4 within 20 months.

Also

Led a team of four through four to five SAN storage data centre recovery exercises, each delivered ahead of schedule, and trained new joiners along the way.
