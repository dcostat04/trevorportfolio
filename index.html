Icestasy · FlagshipJun 2026 – now · Pitched, specced, built, launched

Building the system an ice cream company runs on

Three connected products, covering sales orders, production and HR, used every day by sales, kitchen, factory and office staff.

Problem
Orders from cafés, hotels, retailers and distributors in six regions arrived as WhatsApp messages. Salespeople re-typed each one, which took 10–15 minutes, and production had no live view of what had been ordered. Stock planning was guesswork.
Users
15–20 salespeople and six regional heads; kitchen and factory staff; all 18 employees for HR.
My role
I noticed the problem, pitched the fix to the owner, wrote the specs, built the products with AI coding tools, rolled them out and trained the team.
Decisions & trade-offs
  • Kept WhatsApp as the starting point. Clients didn't have to change anything. Salespeople paste the message and an AI parser turns it into a structured order.
  • Never let a guess become a wrong order. When a flavour name is ambiguous, the salesperson picks from the options. A keyword-based fallback keeps orders flowing if the AI service is down.
  • Connected sales and production. Weekly ingredient needs are calculated from the last six weeks of real orders, and items are flagged when stock drops below about 2.5 weeks of cover.
  • Recorded every stock movement instead of editable totals, so any number can be traced back and audited.
What shipped
Order Desk: orders, city-based pricing for six regions, GST invoices, sales dashboard and seven reports. Icestasy Ops: receiving, mixing, filling and dispatch, with live stock levels and low-stock alerts. HRMS: attendance, automated leave balances and approvals.
Next time
I'd agree on success measures before launch. I measured the time saved per order clearly, but tracked the drop in stock-outs only informally.

How I work

What I've learned shipping for real users

Start where users already are

Salespeople lived in WhatsApp, so Order Desk starts from a pasted WhatsApp message. Adoption is easier when people don't have to change habits.

Plan for things going wrong

A fallback when the AI service fails, a choice step when a match is unclear, and a confirmation before anything important. Trust is easy to lose.

Ship small, then widen

The first alerting release covered a few arrays. Order Desk started with order entry and grew into invoicing and reporting.

Measure what changed

Minutes per order, hours saved per week, response time. If I can't measure it, I can't argue for the next step.
